What is balance?
Balance is the body's ability to maintain its center of mass over its base of support — a continuous, automatic process that most of us never consciously think about.

It depends on the real-time integration of three sensory systems: the visual system (what your eyes tell you about the world), the vestibular system (your inner ear's sense of gravity and motion), and proprioception (feedback from muscles and joints about body position).

The brain synthesises all three streams and sends corrective motor commands to muscles every fraction of a second. Any disruption to this loop — whether from age, injury, or neurological disease — impairs balance.
⚠️
Balance with age
Balance gradually declines with age as sensory acuity, muscle strength, and neural processing speed all reduce. This gradual change makes it a major risk factor for falls in the elderly — falls are one of the leading causes of injury-related death in older adults worldwide.

Research now suggests that subtle changes in balance and gait may be among the earliest detectable signs of neurological conditions such as Parkinson's disease, often appearing years before a clinical diagnosis is made.

Despite this, the existing Indian balance database is currently zero. There is no normative data on how balance changes with age, gender, or lifestyle within the Indian population — meaning clinicians in India have no local reference point to assess whether a patient's balance is normal for their age.
💡
Good balance as we age
The encouraging news is that balance decline is not inevitable. With early detection and targeted intervention, balance can be meaningfully improved at almost any age.

Simple, evidence-based exercises — balance training, tai-chi, strength work — have been shown to significantly reduce fall risk and slow the progression of balance-related decline when started early enough.

The Sthir study aims to build the normative reference data that makes early detection possible for Indian populations, enabling clinicians to flag at-risk individuals before a fall or diagnosis occurs — and giving people the chance to act while it still makes a difference.

Invitation

We invite you to participate in our research study as a volunteer, however before you decide to participate, it is critical that you understand our research and your role as a volunteer. Please read the following sections carefully and discuss with us about any doubt or additional information that you may require to make an independent informed decision.

Background to the Study

As people age, their balance gradually weakens making them more likely to fall — that can cause bone fractures and muscle-joint injuries. These injuries severely affect mobility and overall well-being of elderly as the recovery is slow and inefficient. We all know of some relative, friend or acquaintance who suffered such a fall. New research indicates that poor balance in elderly may correlate with higher chances of neurodegenerative diseases such as Parkinson's disease and Alzheimer's disease. Early detection of balance issues and active intervention can significantly improve the quality of life in elderly and in those with neurodegeneration.

Motivation of the Study

If we can understand factors that affect balance, we can likely prevent a large number of falls in elderly people. Injuries caused by these falls are a major reason for disability, dependence and poor quality of life in old age. We have developed a balance measuring platform, which can — just like a weighing scale — measure balance in healthy individuals and in patients with balance problems. The purpose of this study is to measure balance in a large number of people of different age, gender and lifestyles — to understand factors that affect balance in Indian population.

Can I participate and help?

Yes, we are looking for volunteers to participate in our academic study and help us create a database for balance in Indian population. Anyone who is interested in understanding their own balance and would like to contribute to science and society can participate in the study.

The datasets will include: Healthy individuals — volunteers with no history of fall; At risk — volunteers with a history of unintentional fall but no clinically diagnosed balance disorder; Patients — volunteers with clinically diagnosed balance disorder — each further split into Urban and Rural.

Do I have to participate?

Participation in this study is entirely voluntary. You are free to decline to participate without giving any reasons. Even if you consent now, you can withdraw from the study at any time, without any consequences. Though we would like to know the reasons for your withdrawal.

What will happen if I agree to participate?

On receiving your informed consent, we will schedule an appointment for your visit to our research laboratory on a mutually suitable date and time. In the research laboratory, we will ask for some details about your health and lifestyle, relevant to the project, to label your data effectively (10–15 mins) (questionnaire 2). Then we will explain and ask you to stand on our force platform, as one stands on a weighing scale. We will measure your balance in different stances, likely 5–6 stances (1 min each), ensuring your comfort and safety. In total in the research laboratory, it will take about 30 mins to explain, collect details and measure balance.

What if I do not want to answer some of the questions?

It is your choice not to answer some of the questions and we will respect your choice. It is important for us that whichever question you choose to answer, you answer it truest to your knowledge. It will be detrimental to the study if you provide misleading answer(s).

Will I have to visit many times?

The baseline measurement for balance will be measured only once. At the end of the measurement, we will ask you if you want to participate in a follow-up or intervention study. According to your response, we will indicate if you need to come again, with details.

Will any invasive procedure be performed on me? / Are there any risks?

No, your participation involves only answering a few questions and standing on a platform for a few minutes. This is a completely non-invasive study and does not involve any risk beyond day-to-day standing on both legs or one leg.

What are the benefits of participating?

Your balance data is critical for us to understand factors that impact balance in Indian populations. You may not derive any direct or immediate material benefit from the study, but your data will help us build technologies and interventions to improve balance in the coming years. Volunteer participation is fundamental to advance human health research — every medication and medical device we use today to lead healthy and comfortable lives was once tested on volunteers.

Will my information and data be confidential?

Yes, your information and data will be strictly confidential. Your data will be coded with a linking key and stored in the university databases. Only researchers working on the project, at any given time, will have access to the data. Your data will be stored with no time limit, as long-term data is highly valuable for research purposes.

Can I ask for a copy of my data, or ask you to delete it?

Yes, only you can ask for a copy of your recorded data by raising a request through Form 3, or ask us to delete your data at any time by raising a request through Form 4 — contact the research team and they will assist with subsequent steps. Note: once you request deletion of your data, you will not be able to re-enrol in the study at a later date.

What will happen to the results of the study?

The results will be communicated to the broader research and clinical community through peer-reviewed publications, talks, seminars or reports. No volunteer will be identified in any communication. If you would like to understand your data better, you can contact us for an in-depth analysis as the research advances.

Compensation

You will not receive any monetary compensation for participating in this study.

Who has reviewed this study?

This study has been reviewed and approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Plaksha University.
