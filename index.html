What is balance?
Balance is the body's ability to maintain its center of mass over its base of support — a continuous, automatic process that most of us never consciously think about.

It depends on the real-time integration of three sensory systems: the visual system (what your eyes tell you about the world), the vestibular system (your inner ear's sense of gravity and motion), and proprioception (feedback from muscles and joints about body position).

The brain synthesises all three streams and sends corrective motor commands to muscles every fraction of a second. Any disruption to this loop — whether from age, injury, or neurological disease — impairs balance.
⚠️
Balance with age
Balance gradually declines with age as sensory acuity, muscle strength, and neural processing speed all reduce. This gradual change makes it a major risk factor for falls in the elderly — falls are one of the leading causes of injury-related death in older adults worldwide.

Research now suggests that subtle changes in balance and gait may be among the earliest detectable signs of neurological conditions such as Parkinson's disease, often appearing years before a clinical diagnosis is made.

Despite this, the existing Indian balance database is currently zero. There is no normative data on how balance changes with age, gender, or lifestyle within the Indian population — meaning clinicians in India have no local reference point to assess whether a patient's balance is normal for their age.
💡
Good balance as we age
The encouraging news is that balance decline is not inevitable. With early detection and targeted intervention, balance can be meaningfully improved at almost any age.

Simple, evidence-based exercises — balance training, tai-chi, strength work — have been shown to significantly reduce fall risk and slow the progression of balance-related decline when started early enough.

The Sthir study aims to build the normative reference data that makes early detection possible for Indian populations, enabling clinicians to flag at-risk individuals before a fall or diagnosis occurs — and giving people the chance to act while it still makes a difference.
